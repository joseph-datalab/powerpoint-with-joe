# How I Approach a Presentation

## Understand the goal

Before working on the slides, I first look at what the presentation is supposed to achieve.

Who is going to see it?

What do they need to understand?

What decision needs to be made?

Those questions help me decide what should and shouldn't be in the deck.

## Organize the information

I break the information down and arrange it in an order that makes sense.

I don't want someone to reach slide 10 before they understand what the presentation is actually about.

## Keep the message clear

I try to avoid filling slides with unnecessary text.

When the information is complicated, I break it into shorter points, charts, numbers, comparisons, or simple visuals.

## Design the slides

Once the story is clear, I work on the visual side.

I pay attention to:

- Layout
- Typography
- Spacing
- Alignment
- Colors
- Charts
- Visual hierarchy
- Consistency

The goal is to make the important information easy to notice without making the slide feel busy.

## Review everything

Before I consider a presentation finished, I go through the deck again.

I check:

- Spelling and grammar
- Numbers and labels
- Font consistency
- Alignment
- Spacing
- Chart titles
- Data units
- Colors
- Slide titles
- Repeated elements
- Overall slide-to-slide consistency

Small mistakes can make a good presentation look unfinished, so this part matters to me.
